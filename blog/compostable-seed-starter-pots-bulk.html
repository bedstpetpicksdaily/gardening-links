Frequently Asked Questions

Are compostable seed starter pots safe for vegetables and herbs?

Yes, compostable seed starter pots are safe for all vegetables and herbs since they're made from natural materials like peat, coir, or plant fibers without harmful chemicals. You can plant the entire pot directly into soil, and it will break down within 4-6 weeks, providing organic matter to your garden bed.

How long do compostable seed pots take to decompose in soil?

Most compostable seed starter pots decompose within 4-6 weeks when planted directly in moist soil, though decomposition time varies based on pot thickness, soil moisture, and temperature. Thinner pots in warm, wet conditions break down faster, while cooler or drier conditions may extend the timeline to 8-12 weeks.

What size compostable pots should I buy in bulk for starting seeds?

For most seed starting, 2-inch pots are ideal for individual seedlings, while 3-inch pots work better for slower-growing plants like peppers. If you're starting hundreds of seeds, buying bulk assortments with multiple sizes (1.5-3 inches) gives you flexibility without excess waste.

Do I need to remove compostable seed pots before transplanting or can I plant them directly?

You can plant compostable pots directly into soil or larger containers without removing them—this is their main advantage over plastic. However, tear or perforate the pot's sides and especially the bottom to ensure roots can penetrate easily, and make sure the pot rim stays below soil level so it decomposes properly.
