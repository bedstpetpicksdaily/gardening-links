What to Look For in Shade Cloth With Grommets

Shade Percentage (30%, 50%, or 70%)

30% cloth is ideal for most vegetables and herbs, blocking excessive sun while maintaining light for photosynthesis. 50% works for extremely heat-sensitive plants like lettuce and shade-lovers. Don't exceed 70% unless you're protecting nursery plants or seedlings—too much blocking stunts growth. Check your specific plants' requirements before purchasing.

UV-Stabilized Material (3+ Year Lifespan)

Cheap shade cloth degrades in one season under intense sun. Look for HDPE (high-density polyethylene) material specifically labeled "UV stabilized" or guaranteeing 3+ years of use. The grommet attachment points are also critical—poorly reinforced grommets tear out in wind, defeating the entire purpose.

Grommet Quality and Spacing

Grommets every 2-3 feet allow secure fastening without sagging or billowing in wind. Metal grommets outlast plastic, and reinforced edges prevent tearing. Count the grommet spacing on product photos before buying—sparse grommets mean your cloth won't hang properly and will flap against plants, causing damage.

Roll Size and Weight

Shade cloth rolls typically come in 6-foot or 12-foot widths and 50-100 foot lengths. Measure your garden space first and add 20% extra for overlap and securing. Heavier cloth (higher gsm/gram per square meter) hangs better and lasts longer, but requires sturdier support structures. Calculate the total coverage you need before ordering—returning extra footage wastes precious days.
