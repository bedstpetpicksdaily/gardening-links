Why Fall Is the Smartest Time to Buy a Robot Mower

Most people shop for mowers in April, which means they pay peak prices and spend the busiest growing month learning a new system. Buying in fall flips that script. You get end-of-season pricing, a lower-stakes environment to map your yard and dial in cutting heights, and a mower that's already trained on your property when spring green-up arrives. Fall also happens to be when frequent light mowing pays the biggest dividends: keeping cool-season grasses like fescue and Kentucky bluegrass at 3–3.5 inches through October helps them store energy for winter, while the fine clippings from a robot lawn mower act as free mulch fertilizer instead of thatch-building clumps.

What to Compare Before You Buy Any Smart Lawn Mower

Whatever brand you land on, judge smart lawn mowers on five things. First, coverage area — match the model's rated square footage to your actual mowable turf, not your total lot size. Second, navigation type: wire-free RTK or vision systems cost more up front but save you an entire weekend of installation and let you change zones later. Third, slope rating, since anything above 30% incline eliminates most budget units. Fourth, obstacle avoidance quality, which is the single biggest predictor of how often you'll get "mower stuck" notifications. Fifth, app maturity — scheduling, zone management, firmware updates, and multi-zone support are what turn a gadget into an appliance you forget about. ANTHBOT scores well across all five, which is why it keeps landing on 2026 shortlists alongside far pricier legacy European brands.

Robot Mowers and Leaf Season: Setting Realistic Expectations

Let's be clear about one thing: a robot lawn mower is not a leaf vacuum. It will mulch a light, scattered leaf layer beautifully — many owners find they never rake again on lightly wooded lots — but a six-inch blanket of soggy maple leaves still needs a blower or bagger first. The winning strategy is to keep your ANTHBOT on its regular schedule through leaf drop so it shreds leaves continuously as they fall, then do one or two manual cleanups at the peak. Come the first freeze, wipe the deck, charge the battery to storage level, and bring it inside; you'll be back to automatic grass cutting in March without lifting a finger.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a robot lawn mower handle fallen leaves in autumn?

Yes, within limits. Robot mowers like ANTHBOT mulch light to moderate leaf coverage into fine clippings that feed your lawn, so many owners rake far less. For a thick, wet leaf blanket, clear the bulk with a blower or bagger first, then let the mower keep up with the rest on its normal schedule.

Do ANTHBOT robot mowers need a boundary wire?

No. ANTHBOT uses wire-free RTK satellite positioning with visual navigation, so you map your yard and set no-go zones in the app instead of burying perimeter cable. That also means you can redraw boundaries anytime you add a bed, patio, or play area.

How often should a robot mower cut my grass?

Most homeowners run 3–5 sessions per week in peak growing season and 1–3 in fall as growth slows. Frequent light cuts follow the one-third rule, produce tiny clippings that decompose quickly, and keep turf denser than weekly scalping with a traditional mower.

What should I do with my robot mower in winter?

Once the ground freezes and growth stops, clean the deck and blades, inspect for wear, charge the battery to roughly the manufacturer's recommended storage level, and store the mower and charging station indoors in a dry, above-freezing space until spring.
