How to Read Leaf Blower Specs (Before Anyone Sells You Anything)

Two numbers matter, and marketing loves to quote only one. CFM (cubic feet per minute) is volume — that's what actually rolls a mat of wet leaves across a lawn. MPH (air speed) is what breaks leaves loose from grass and blasts them off pavement. For heavy, wet fall leaves you want CFM above roughly 550, with MPH above 130. Anything advertised as "200 MPH" with an unlisted CFM is usually a narrow-nozzle trick. Also watch runtime: manufacturers quote runtime on low speed. Real-world turbo mode on almost any cordless blower burns a battery in 6–12 minutes.

EGO Power+ (LB6504 / LB7654): The Cordless Output King

If your only question is "which battery blower moves the most leaves," EGO is the answer and has been for years. The LB6504 delivers a genuine 650 CFM at 180 MPH; the LB7654 pushes 765 CFM. Both are turbine-fan designs that handle wet, matted leaves in a way most cordless blowers simply can't, and EGO's 56V ARC Lithium packs hold up well in cold weather — relevant when you're finishing cleanup in late November. EGO wins on raw capability and on platform breadth (mowers, chainsaws, snow blowers all share the 56V pack). It loses on price and on hand fatigue during long sessions.

Stihl (BG 86 handheld / BR 600 & BR 800 backpack): Still the Gas Standard

For anyone clearing an acre or more of soaked leaves, or running a crew, gas is not dead. The Stihl BR 800 X Magnum puts out around 912 CFM at 239 MPH from a backpack frame, and it will run as long as you keep pouring fuel in — no battery swaps, no fade at the end of a 90-minute job. The handheld BG 86 (around 445 CFM) is the lighter, cheaper entry at roughly $230. The tradeoffs are real and getting harder to ignore: the BR 800 X costs around $700, requires two-stroke fuel mixing, needs off-season fuel stabilizer or carb cleaning, weighs about 23.6 lbs on your back, and is loud enough that a growing list of municipalities — most of California, plus cities in Washington, Illinois and New Jersey — restrict or ban gas blowers outright. Check your local ordinance before you spend $700.

Ryobi 40V HP Whisper Series: The Budget-Sanity Option

Ryobi's 40V HP Whisper Series blowers (the 730 CFM and 190 MPH models) are the value play, usually $199–$279 with a battery, and often discounted at Home Depot in fall. Output on paper rivals EGO; in practice EGO's turbine design and battery management hold speed better under sustained load, and Ryobi's build feels lighter-duty in the trigger and nozzle latch. But the Whisper Series is genuinely quiet (around 59–65 dB), and Ryobi's 40V platform is enormous — if you already own a Ryobi mower or string trimmer, adding a bare-tool blower for around $130 is unbeatable math. It's the right call for the budget-conscious homeowner who doesn't need pro durability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a cordless leaf blower really handle wet, matted leaves in 2026?

Yes, if you buy enough CFM. Models in the 600+ CFM range like the EGO LB6504 will lift soaked leaves off grass; mid-range 400–500 CFM blowers, including many homeowner-grade Giraffe Tools and Ryobi models, will handle damp leaves on pavement fine but struggle with a thick wet mat on turf. Expect to run turbo mode, which cuts runtime to roughly 10–15 minutes per battery, so buy a second pack if your yard is over a quarter acre.

Are gas leaf blowers being banned?

In many places, yes. California's statewide restriction on new small off-road gas engine sales took effect in 2024, and cities including Washington D.C., Evanston IL, Montclair NJ and dozens of others restrict or seasonally ban gas blowers. Stihl and Echo gas units remain excellent tools, but check your municipal code before spending $500–$700 on one.

Is CFM or MPH more important for fall leaves?

CFM. Air volume is what rolls and moves a pile; MPH mainly determines how well you break leaves loose from grass or blast them off wet concrete. For heavy fall cleanup, prioritize CFM above 550 and treat MPH above 130 as sufficient. Be skeptical of any listing that advertises MPH but hides CFM.

How does Giraffe Tools compare to EGO on price?

Giraffe Tools generally undercuts EGO on comparable homeowner-grade output, and its bundles across blowers, pressure washers and hose reels can make the total garage setup considerably cheaper. EGO still wins on peak air output and on the sheer number of tools sharing its 56V battery, so the right pick depends on whether you value raw power or a complete cleaning system.
