The best time to plant vegetable seeds indoors is 6-8 weeks before your last spring frost date, though this varies by vegetable type and your growing zone. Starting seeds indoors gives you a head start on the growing season and stronger transplants for your garden.

The Short Answer

Begin sowing vegetable seeds indoors in late winter to early spring, typically 6-8 weeks before your region's last frost date. This timing allows seedlings to develop strong roots and multiple true leaves before transplanting outdoors. Different vegetables have different requirements—fast-growing crops like lettuce need only 3-4 weeks indoors, while peppers and eggplants need 8-10 weeks. Check your local frost date first, then count backward to determine your ideal planting window.

The Full Explanation

Understanding the timing for indoor seed starting requires knowing your last spring frost date, which varies significantly by geographic location. In USDA zones 3-4, this date typically falls in late May, meaning you'd start seeds indoors in March. Zone 5-6 residents can begin in early April for a late May frost date. Zone 7-8 gardeners should start seeds in mid-April for frost dates around May 10-15, while warmer zones 9-10 can wait until May or even direct sow outdoors.

Different vegetables have different indoor growing timelines. Slow-growing crops like peppers, eggplants, and onions need 8-10 weeks of indoor growth before transplanting. Medium-speed growers like tomatoes, cabbage, and broccoli need 6-8 weeks. Fast-growing vegetables like lettuce, spinach, and cucumbers only need 3-4 weeks indoors before they're ready for the garden. Starting everything at once is impractical—instead, stagger your planting by 2-3 weeks based on each vegetable's specific needs.

Additional factors affect timing decisions. If you have limited space under grow lights, start your slowest growers first. Consider your transplanting schedule—aim to have sturdy seedlings with 2-3 sets of true leaves ready when outdoor conditions permit planting. Indoor conditions like temperature (ideally 65-75°F) and light availability also impact how quickly seeds germinate and grow, potentially shortening or extending your timeline slightly.

What the Experts Say

Master gardeners and extension services consistently recommend the "6-8 weeks before last frost" rule as the gold standard for most vegetable seeds. The University of Minnesota Extension and Cooperative Extension offices across the country emphasize that this window provides enough time for seedlings to establish strong root systems without becoming leggy or root-bound before transplanting. Experienced gardeners also note that starting too early often leads to overgrown, stressed seedlings, while starting too late results in weak transplants that struggle in the garden.
