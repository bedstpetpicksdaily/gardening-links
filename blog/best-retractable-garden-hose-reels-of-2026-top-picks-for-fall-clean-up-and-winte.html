Why a Retractable Hose Reel Matters Most in Fall and Winter

A loose hose left on the ground in autumn is a trip hazard buried under leaves, a magnet for standing water, and a prime candidate for freeze damage. Water trapped inside a hose expands as it freezes, splitting the inner liner and creating pinhole leaks you won't discover until spring. A retractable reel solves this by keeping the hose fully drained, coiled and off the ground — usually mounted to a wall, fence post or garage exterior where it stays out of the mud and away from standing puddles. The retraction mechanism also removes the number-one cause of hose failure: repeated kinking and sharp bends at the tap. For fall clean-up specifically, being able to pull out 30 or 50 feet, blast down the driveway, and let it rewind in seconds means you'll actually finish the job instead of putting it off.

How to Winterise a Retractable Hose Reel Properly

Do this before the first hard frost, not after. Shut off the water supply at the tap and disconnect the leader hose. Pull the full length of hose out, hold the nozzle open and let gravity drain it completely — walking the hose from one end helps push residual water out. Allow it to dry, then let it retract fully. If you live somewhere with sustained sub-freezing temperatures, unclip the reel from its wall bracket and store it in a shed, garage or basement; if winters are mild, leaving it mounted is fine, though a simple cover extends the life of the casing. Finally, insulate or cover the outdoor spigot itself. Ten minutes of prep in November saves you a replacement hose in March.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I leave a retractable hose reel outside all winter?

In mild climates, yes — as long as the hose is fully drained and the water supply is disconnected. In regions with sustained hard freezes, it's better to drain the hose, let it retract, then unclip the reel from its wall bracket and store it in a garage or shed. Hoselink reels are designed to lift off their bracket in seconds, which makes this easy.

What length hose reel should I buy?

Measure the distance from your outdoor tap to the furthest point you need to reach, then add about 15 percent for slack and manoeuvring. Most small to mid-sized yards are well served by a 30 to 50 foot reel, while larger properties or those with a single tap may need a longer option or a second reel mounted elsewhere.

Do retractable hose reels kink like regular hoses?

Far less. Because the hose is wound onto a drum under even tension and pulled out in a straight line, it avoids the tight loops and sharp bends at the tap that cause most kinking. Quality matters, though — reels with kink-resistant hose and a smooth, controlled rewind mechanism, like Hoselink's, perform noticeably better than budget spring-loaded models.
