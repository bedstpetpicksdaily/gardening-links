Traverseon Portable Air Conditioning Units

Traverseon's portable air conditioners are engineered for RV owners who need flexibility without permanent installation. Their models typically range from 8,000 to 14,000 BTU, with price points between $350–$650. A key advantage is portability: you can move the unit between rooms or take it on camping trips to different vehicles. Traverseon units feature window installation kits, remote controls, and energy efficiency ratings around 10.5 EER (Energy Efficiency Ratio). However, portable units require a window or vent opening to expel hot air, which can limit where you can place them in compact RV layouts. Installation takes about 30 minutes, and noise levels typically range from 65–72 decibels.

Dometic RoofTop Air Conditioning (Traditional Built-In)

Dometic is the gold standard for traditional RV air conditioning. Their rooftop units (Brisk Air series) range from 13,500 to 15,000 BTU and cost $1,200–$2,000, plus $500–$800 for professional installation. Once installed, they're essentially silent in the cabin, cool much faster than portable units, and don't sacrifice interior space. The major drawbacks: installation is permanent, requires roof penetration (which can lead to leaks if not sealed properly), and repairs often mean visiting an RV service center. Dometic units are highly efficient but their upfront cost and installation complexity make them a commitment rather than a flexible solution.

EdgeStar Portable Air Conditioner

EdgeStar's portable AC line is a close competitor to Traverseon, offering 8,000–12,000 BTU models at similar price points ($300–$600). EdgeStar units are known for excellent customer reviews and slightly quieter operation (62–68 dB) compared to Traverseon. However, EdgeStar portable units have lower cooling capacity than Dometic's installed systems and, like Traverseon, still require window or vent space. EdgeStar's warranty is typically 1 year; Traverseon generally matches this. EdgeStar is slightly more compact, which RV owners often prefer, but Traverseon models typically offer better integration with power stations and solar setups popular in the RV community.

Our Verdict

The verdict depends on your RV lifestyle: If you own a smaller RV (Class B or C), camp frequently in different locations, or want to avoid permanent modifications, a Traverseon portable unit is the better choice—it's affordable, flexible, and pairs beautifully with Traverseon's ecosystem of power stations for true off-grid comfort. If you have a large motorhome, plan to stay in one rig long-term, and want the fastest, most silent cooling with no interior space impact, invest in a traditional Dometic rooftop system—the upfront cost is justified by permanent efficiency. For budget-conscious boondockers who want quiet performance without permanent installation, EdgeStar is competitive with Traverseon but offers slightly less integration with solar/power solutions. Many experienced RV owners choose Traverseon for a second portable unit to supplement their primary system, combining the best of both worlds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a Traverseon portable AC while boondocking off-grid?

Yes, Traverseon portable units draw 1,000–1,500 watts and work directly with Traverseon power stations or solar generators, making them ideal for off-grid camping. A traditional rooftop system requires 15+ amps of 120V shore power, limiting boondocking flexibility.

How long does it take to cool an RV with a portable AC vs rooftop system?

A 14,000 BTU Traverseon portable AC typically cools a Class B RV in 45–60 minutes. A Dometic rooftop system cools the same space in 15–25 minutes. The difference matters during extreme heat but is negligible for overnight cooling.

Will installing a traditional rooftop AC void my RV warranty?

Possibly. Some manufacturers require certified installation, and roof penetration can affect weatherproofing. Traverseon portable units involve no roof work, eliminating this risk entirely.

How loud are Traverseon portable AC units?

Traverseon models operate at 65–72 dB, comparable to a conversation. EdgeStar is slightly quieter (62–68 dB). Traditional rooftop systems are quieter in the cabin (compressor noise is external) but require the outdoor condenser unit to run.

Can I use a portable AC in an RV without a window or roof vent?

No. Portable ACs require an exhaust vent to expel hot air. You'll need a window kit or a through-wall vent. This is a major limitation compared to traditional systems in compact RV layouts.
