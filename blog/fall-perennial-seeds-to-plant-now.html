What to Look For

1. Cold Stratification Requirements

Check the seed packet for stratification needs. Seeds that require cold stratification are actually ideal for fall planting—they'll get the chilling period naturally over winter. Look for packets marked "needs winter cold" or "direct sow in fall." This is a feature, not a bug, and means higher germination rates when spring arrives.

2. Hardiness Zone Compatibility

Verify that all seeds in your collection are rated for your USDA hardiness zone. An autumn mix is only valuable if every variety survives your winters. Check the package for zone ranges (typically listed as "Zones 3-9" or similar). Mismatched zones mean dead plants come spring—a expensive lesson.

3. Bloom Timeline Diversity

The best autumn mixes include early bloomers (April-May), mid-season (June-July), and late bloomers (August-September). This ensures continuous color throughout the growing season. Look for collections that explicitly list bloom times for each variety, not vague promises of "spring and summer color."

4. Germination Rate and Seed Freshness

Purchase from retailers with current-year seeds (look for harvest dates on packets). Germination rates should be listed at 70% or higher. Older seeds from last season may still be available at discount, but lower germination means wasted effort. For fall planting specifically, buy the freshest seeds available.
