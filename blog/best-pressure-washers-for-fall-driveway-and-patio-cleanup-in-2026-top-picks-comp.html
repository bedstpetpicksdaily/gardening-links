Fall is the hardest season on hard surfaces. Wet leaves sit on concrete for a week and leave tannin stains that look like coffee spills. Algae and moss bloom on the shady side of a patio once temperatures drop into the 50s. Add pollen residue, grill grease and the black spot mildew that shows up under tree cover, and by November most driveways look a full shade darker than they did in July. A pressure washer is the fastest fix — but which one you buy depends far more on your driveway size and storage situation than on the PSI number printed on the box.

Giraffe Tools Grandfalls Pressure Washer — Best for Repeat Use and Zero Setup Friction

The Grandfalls is the odd one out in this group because it isn't a cart — it's a wall-mounted unit with a retractable hose reel that you bolt to your garage wall and plug into a standard 120V outlet. Depending on configuration you get roughly 1800–2300 PSI at about 1.8–2.0 GPM, with a 100-foot retractable hose that covers most two-car driveways plus a patio without moving the machine. Prices generally land in the $400–$600 range depending on model and hose length, which is real money next to a $160 Sun Joe.

What you're paying for is the elimination of setup. The single biggest reason pressure washers sit unused is the 12-minute ritual of dragging a cart out, uncoiling hose, hooking up the garden line and then reversing it all. With the Grandfalls, you pull the hose, connect the water line once (it stays connected), and go. For fall cleanup — where you often want a 10-minute spot pass on leaf stains rather than a full Saturday session — that changes how often the thing actually gets used. Build quality is solid: metal fittings, a decent induction-style motor, and an adjustable pressure dial on the unit itself. The trade-off is that it's fixed to one location. It will not go with you to clean a boat at the ramp or help a friend's driveway across town.

Sun Joe SPX3000 — Best Budget Option Under $200

The SPX3000 has been the default entry-level electric washer for close to a decade, and at roughly $150–$180 it remains hard to argue with. It's rated 2030 PSI and 1.76 GPM, includes five quick-connect nozzle tips, and has dual onboard detergent tanks — genuinely useful for fall, where you'll want a dedicated mildew/algae cleaner for shaded patio pavers. For a small townhouse driveway or a 300-square-foot patio, it does the job.

The honest downsides: the 20-foot high-pressure hose and 35-foot power cord mean you'll be repositioning the unit constantly on anything larger than a single-car pad, and the hose is stiff PVC that kinks in cold weather. The plastic wand and inlet fittings are the usual failure points — many owners replace the inlet connector within two seasons. It's a universal motor rather than induction, so it's louder and rated for shorter continuous run times. Buy it if your total hard surface is small or you use a washer three times a year.

Ryobi RY142300 (2300 PSI Brushless) — Best Portable Electric

Ryobi's 2300 PSI brushless electric, typically $350–$400 at Home Depot, sits between the Sun Joe and the gas units. The brushless induction motor is noticeably quieter and longer-lived than the Sun Joe's, it delivers about 1.2 GPM, and the upright cart design with onboard hose reel and 25-foot hose is the best-executed portable layout in this group. The turbo nozzle it ships with strips concrete stains faster than a standard 15-degree tip.

Where it loses to the Grandfalls is flow rate and reach — 1.2 GPM is meaningfully slower than 1.8–2.0 GPM when you're covering 800 square feet of driveway, and you'll still be dragging the cart and coiling hose every time. Where it beats the Grandfalls is portability and the fact that you can store it in a corner instead of drilling into your garage wall. Renters should look here first.

Simpson MegaShot MS60763 — Best for Large Driveways and Stubborn Stains

If your driveway is long, gravel-adjacent, or hasn't been cleaned in five years, gas still wins on raw throughput. The Simpson MegaShot with a Honda GC190 engine runs about $400–$450 and delivers 3200 PSI at 2.4 GPM. That GPM figure is the important one: it's roughly 25% more water per minute than the Grandfalls and double the Ryobi, which translates directly into square feet cleaned per hour. Surface cleaner attachments work far better behind a gas unit.

The costs are real, though. It's loud enough to annoy neighbors, requires oil changes, needs pump saver or full winterization before the first hard freeze, and 3200 PSI will absolutely etch soft concrete, damage mortar joints and shred wood decking if you use the wrong tip. For a typical suburban patio and two-car driveway, it's more machine than the job needs.

Head-to-Head: What Actually Matters in Fall

For leaf tannin and algae, chemistry beats pressure — a sodium hypochlorite or oxygenated cleaner with a 5-minute dwell time does more than an extra 1000 PSI. That favors machines with usable detergent delivery: the Sun Joe's dual tanks and the Grandfalls' onboard soap system both work well. For coverage speed on big surfaces, GPM rules, which favors Simpson then Giraffe Tools. For cold weather, all electrics need the pump drained; the Grandfalls has an advantage here because it lives in a heated or semi-heated garage rather than an unheated shed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 2000 PSI enough for a concrete driveway, or do I need a gas washer?

For routine fall cleanup — leaf tannin, algae, pollen and light mildew — 1800–2300 PSI with 1.8+ GPM is plenty, especially paired with a surface cleaner attachment and a dwell-time detergent. Gas units like the Simpson MegaShot (3200 PSI, 2.4 GPM) mainly buy you speed on very large areas and deeply embedded oil or rust stains, not fundamentally better results. On soft or older concrete, 3200 PSI can actually etch the surface if you hold the wand too close.

How do I winterize an electric pressure washer like the Grandfalls or Sun Joe?

Disconnect the garden hose, run the unit for a few seconds to purge remaining water, squeeze the trigger to release pressure, then pump antifreeze-based pump saver into the inlet until it comes out the outlet. Any water left inside the pump will freeze, expand and crack the head. A wall-mounted unit in an attached garage is somewhat more forgiving than a cart stored in an unheated shed, but you should still run pump saver before the first hard freeze.

Should I use a surface cleaner attachment on a patio?

Yes for flat concrete and large pavers — a rotating surface cleaner gives you even results in a fraction of the time and eliminates the zebra-stripe wand marks that plague first-time users. Skip it on loose-jointed pavers, soft brick or anything with crumbling mortar, and match the attachment to your machine's GPM; a 2.4 GPM gas unit will spin a 15-inch cleaner properly, while a 1.2 GPM electric usually needs a 11–12 inch model to maintain rotation.
