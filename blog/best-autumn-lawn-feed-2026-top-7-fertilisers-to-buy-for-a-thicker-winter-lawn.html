As the days shorten and the first frosts creep across British gardens, your lawn quietly shifts gear. Summer growth slows, roots start hunting for reserves, and moss and disease begin eyeing up any weak patches. This is exactly why autumn feeding matters so much: the right fertiliser applied between late August and early November can be the difference between a tired, patchy sward in March and a dense, deep-green lawn that shrugs off winter.

The problem is that most gardeners reach for whatever bag is left over from spring — and that's a mistake. High-nitrogen summer feeds push soft, sappy top growth that frost and fungal disease absolutely love. Autumn lawn feeds flip the formula, leaning heavily on potassium and phosphate to harden cell walls, thicken roots and prepare the grass plant for months of cold, wet dormancy.

Why Autumn Lawn Feed Is Different (And Why It Matters)

A true autumn or winter lawn fertiliser is built around a low-nitrogen, high-potassium NPK ratio — something like 4-6-12 or 3-10-20 rather than the 12-4-6 you'd use in May. Nitrogen drives leaf growth; potassium (potash) drives hardiness, drought and frost tolerance, and disease resistance. Phosphate feeds root development, which is what really thickens a lawn from the ground up. Many autumn blends also include iron sulphate, which deepens colour, hardens the leaf and knocks back moss at the same time — a huge bonus going into the wettest months of the year. Apply at the right rate on moist soil and you'll see colour improve within 7–14 days, with the real payoff arriving next spring when your lawn greens up weeks ahead of the neighbours'.

How to Apply Autumn Lawn Feed Correctly

Mow the lawn a couple of days beforehand, leaving it slightly longer than summer height (around 4cm). Scarify and aerate if thatch or compaction is an issue — this lets the feed reach the root zone. Apply granules with a spreader for even coverage, ideally when the soil is moist but the grass leaf is dry, and always stick to the stated grams per square metre; over-application scorches turf and wastes product. Water in if no rain is forecast within 48 hours. If you're combining feed with an iron-based moss treatment, expect the moss to blacken within a week — rake it out afterwards and overseed any bare patches while the soil is still warm.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to apply autumn lawn feed in the UK?

Between early September and mid-November, while the soil is still warm enough for root activity. Aim to apply at least four to six weeks before the first hard frosts, on moist soil with dry grass leaves.

Can I use my normal spring lawn feed in autumn?

It's not advisable. Spring feeds are high in nitrogen, which forces soft leafy growth that is easily damaged by frost and prone to fungal disease. Choose a low-nitrogen, high-potassium autumn formula instead.

Will autumn lawn feed kill moss?

A standard autumn feed won't, but formulations containing iron sulphate will blacken and weaken moss within about a week. Rake out the dead moss afterwards and overseed bare patches while the soil is still warm.
