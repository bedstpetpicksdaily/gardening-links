Step 1: Adjust Your Mowing Schedule and Cutting Height for Fall

Grass growth slows dramatically once soil temperatures drop, so running your mower on a peak-summer schedule wastes battery cycles and compacts damp turf. Cut back to two or three sessions per week and shift them to mid-day or early afternoon when dew has burned off — wet grass clumps, sticks to the deck, and dulls blades faster. Raise your cutting height by 10–15mm as well; slightly longer blades help the lawn photosynthesize through shorter days and insulate crowns against early frost. With an ANTHBOT robot mower, these changes take seconds in the companion app, and you can set zone-specific schedules so shaded, slow-growing areas get mowed less often than sunny open turf.

Step 2: Turn Leaf Litter Into Free Mulch (Instead of a Blockage)

A robotic lawn mower's small, fast-spinning blades are surprisingly effective leaf mulchers when leaf cover is light to moderate. Passing over a thin scattering of dry leaves shreds them into fine confetti that filters down to the soil and feeds your lawn through winter — free fertilizer, no raking. The key is frequency and dryness: mow before leaf drop builds into a thick mat, and always on dry days. If leaves are ankle-deep or soaked from rain, rake or blow the heaviest piles off first, then send the robot out to finish. ANTHBOT models with strong drive motors and obstacle-avoidance vision handle scattered debris confidently, but no robot mower is a substitute for a leaf blower when your maples dump everything in one weekend.

Step 3: Deep Clean, Inspect, and Replace Wear Parts

Before storage, give the machine a proper service. Power down fully, then remove caked grass and leaf residue from the underside of the deck using a plastic scraper and stiff brush — avoid pressure washers, which force water into bearings and electronics. Inspect the blades: autumn debris, acorns, and hidden sticks nick edges quickly, and dull blades tear grass instead of slicing it. Swap in a fresh set (they're inexpensive and take minutes), check that blade discs spin freely, and clean the wheels and drive treads of packed mud. Wipe charging contacts on both the mower and base station with a dry cloth, and check the perimeter setup — for RTK-based ANTHBOT units, confirm the antenna mount is secure and free of debris, or store it indoors if your winters are harsh.

Step 4: Battery Care and Safe Winter Storage

Lithium batteries hate two things: deep discharge and freezing temperatures. Charge your mower to roughly 60–80% before storage rather than 100% or empty, then store it in a dry, frost-free space such as a garage, shed with insulation, or utility room — ideally between 5°C and 20°C (40–68°F). Cover it lightly to keep dust off, but don't seal it in plastic where condensation can form. If your storage area dips below freezing, remove the battery pack if your model allows and keep it indoors. Check the charge level once mid-winter and top up if it's fallen below 40%. Finally, take the charging base indoors too if possible, and mark the location of any buried boundary components so you don't spike them with a spade in spring. Come March, a quick blade change, firmware update, and boundary re-check is all it takes to get your ANTHBOT back to fully autonomous lawn care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a robot mower actually mulch fallen leaves?

Yes — within limits. Robotic lawn mowers like ANTHBOT models shred light to moderate layers of dry leaves into fine mulch that feeds the soil. But if leaves are wet or piled thickly, clear the heaviest cover with a rake or blower first, then let the mower finish the job.

When should I stop running my robot mower for the season?

Stop when grass growth effectively halts — typically when soil temperatures stay below about 10°C (50°F), or after the first hard frost. Mowing frozen or frost-covered grass damages the blades of grass and stresses the mower's drive system.

How should I store my robot mower over winter?

Clean it thoroughly, fit new blades, charge the battery to roughly 60–80%, and store it in a dry, frost-free space between 5°C and 20°C. Bring the charging station indoors if possible, and check the battery level once mid-winter, topping up if it falls below 40%.
