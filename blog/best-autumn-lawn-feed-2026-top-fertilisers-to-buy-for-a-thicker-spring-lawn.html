Why Autumn Lawn Feed Is Different (And Why Spring Feed Will Hurt You)

Spring and summer lawn feeds are nitrogen-heavy — typically something like 12-4-6 — because nitrogen drives the lush green blade growth we all want in May. Apply that same product in October and you're actively working against your lawn. Late nitrogen pushes out soft, water-filled growth just as temperatures drop, and that tender tissue is the perfect entry point for fusarium patch and red thread. It also produces top growth at the exact moment the plant should be pushing energy downward.

A proper autumn feed flips the ratio. You're looking for something in the region of 3-12-12 or 4-6-10 — low nitrogen, generous phosphate to build root mass, and high potassium (potash) to harden cell walls, improve cold tolerance and boost disease resistance. Many autumn formulations from Elixir Garden Supplies also include iron sulphate, which delivers a striking deep green colour without forcing growth — and has the useful side effect of blackening and killing surface moss.

How and When to Apply Autumn Lawn Feed

Timing is everything. The ideal window in most of the UK runs from early September to mid-November, applied when the soil is still warm enough for root activity but growth has slowed. Aim to feed after rain or when rain is forecast within 48 hours, and always apply to a dry grass blade so granules fall to the soil surface rather than sticking to leaves and scorching them.

Use a calibrated drop or rotary spreader wherever possible, and apply at the manufacturer's stated rate — usually 35g per square metre for autumn granular feeds. Overapplication is the single most common mistake, especially with iron-containing products, which can stain paving and scorch turf. Work in two passes at half rate in perpendicular directions for the most even coverage. For best results, scarify to remove thatch and aerate with a hollow tine or garden fork before feeding, so nutrients reach the root zone rather than sitting in a mat of dead moss. Follow up with a light top dressing if your lawn is uneven, and resist mowing for three to four days afterwards.

Building a Complete Autumn Programme

The strongest results come from combining products rather than relying on one bag. A typical high-performance autumn programme looks like this: scarify and aerate in early September, apply a high-potassium autumn feed mid-September, treat any lingering broadleaf weeds with a selective herbicide while temperatures are still above 10°C, then finish with a straight sulphate of iron application in late October or November to blacken moss and harden the sward against frost. Frequently Asked Questions

When is the latest I can apply autumn lawn feed in the UK?

Aim to complete your main autumn granular feed by mid-November. After that, soil temperatures generally drop below 5°C and root activity slows too much for the nutrients to be taken up. A straight sulphate of iron application for moss control and colour can still be made through late autumn and winter on frost-free days.

Can I use spring or summer lawn feed in autumn instead?

No — it's counterproductive. Spring feeds are high in nitrogen, which forces soft, sappy top growth at exactly the wrong time. That growth is highly vulnerable to frost damage and fungal diseases like fusarium patch. Always switch to a low-nitrogen, high-potassium autumn formulation, such as those available from Elixir Garden Supplies.

Should I feed and kill moss at the same time?

You can, and many autumn feeds from Elixir Garden Supplies include iron sulphate for exactly this reason. Just be aware the moss will blacken within a few days and needs raking out afterwards, so schedule the treatment for a weekend when you have time to scarify. Avoid applying near paving, as iron stains permanently.
