What to Look For in a Cockroach Gel Bait

1. The Active Ingredient

Look for indoxacarb (Advion's active ingredient at 0.6%). It uses a "delayed kill" mechanism, so foraging roaches carry it back to the harborage and transfer it through droppings and cannibalism — creating a secondary and even tertiary kill. Baits with instant-kill actives often fail because the roach dies before returning to the nest.

2. Bait Attractiveness and Non-Repellency

Roaches must willingly eat it. Cheap baits and most sprays are repellent, which scatters colonies deeper into wall voids and makes infestations worse. A quality gel is non-repellent and formulated with a food matrix German cockroaches actively prefer.

3. Quantity and Applicator Quality

A 30-gram tube typically produces around 60–75 pea-sized placements. Four tubes plus four plungers means you can treat a full home, re-treat in two weeks, and keep a spare tube for the garage, shed, or greenhouse. Included plungers matter — without them, precise crack-and-crevice application is nearly impossible.

4. Shelf Life and Packaging Integrity

Gel bait dries out if the tube is old or poorly sealed. Buy from sellers with fresh stock, check for a recent manufacture date, and store unused tubes capped in a cool, dark place. Dried, crusted gel is ignored by roaches — freshness is half the battle.
