Step 1: Shut Off and Drain Before the Temperature Drops

Start at the source. Turn off the interior shut-off valve that feeds your outdoor spigot (usually in a basement, crawlspace or utility room), then open the outside tap to let residual water escape. If you have frost-free hose bibs, you still need to disconnect the hose — a connected hose traps water against the valve and defeats the frost-free design entirely. Next, disconnect your hose and walk it out to full length on a sloped driveway or lawn, lifting one end at a time to let gravity do the work. For long runs, coiling the hose over your shoulder while walking forward pushes water out ahead of you. Give nozzles, wands and connectors a shake too; the tiny reservoirs inside spray heads are exactly where ice cracks plastic.

Step 3: Store Accessories, Tools and Solar Lights the Right Way

Once the hose is drained, work through the rest of the garden. Empty and invert sprinklers so no water pools in the base, wipe spray nozzles dry and store them indoors. Hand tools benefit from a quick clean, a light coat of oil on metal surfaces, and hanging storage rather than sitting on a damp concrete floor. Hoselink's range of garden tools and watering accessories is designed to be shared across seasons, so keeping them dry now genuinely pays off in March.

Don't forget solar outdoor lights. Short winter days mean weak charging, which can slowly drain and damage rechargeable batteries. Where possible, relocate solar lights to the sunniest, least shaded spots, brush leaves and frost off the panels regularly, or remove and store them with the batteries taken out. Hoselink's solar lighting range holds up well outdoors, but a little seasonal attention keeps the batteries performing at full capacity for years.

Step 4: A Quick Spring-Ready Checklist

Before you close the shed door, run through this: interior valve off, outdoor tap opened and drained, hose fully emptied and retracted or coiled loosely (never tight — tight coils crack in cold), connectors removed and stored indoors, sprinklers inverted and dry, reel casing wiped down or dismounted, tools oiled and hung, solar lights cleaned or stored. Do this once and next spring you'll simply reattach a click-on fitting, turn the tap, and water — instead of shopping for a replacement hose.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need to disconnect my hose if I have a frost-free spigot?

Yes. A connected hose traps water in the valve body, preventing it from draining properly and allowing ice to form right where the pipe enters your home. Always disconnect, even with frost-free bibs.

Can I leave a retractable hose reel outside over winter?

In mild climates, an enclosed reel like Hoselink's can stay mounted as long as the hose is fully drained and the water supply is off. In regions with hard, sustained freezes, use the removable bracket to bring the unit into a garage or shed.

What's the best way to store a garden hose so it doesn't crack?

Drain it completely, then store it loosely coiled or retracted at room-ish temperature — a garage, shed or basement is ideal. Tight coils and sub-zero concrete floors are the two biggest causes of cracked hose linings.
