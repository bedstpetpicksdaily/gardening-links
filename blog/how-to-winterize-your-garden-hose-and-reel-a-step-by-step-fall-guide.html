Step 1: Shut Off and Drain the Water Supply

Start at the source. Locate the indoor shut-off valve that feeds your outdoor spigot and close it completely. Then head outside, open the tap fully, and let gravity do its job — you want every drop out of that supply line. If you have a frost-free sillcock, disconnecting the hose is non-negotiable: leaving a hose attached traps water inside the valve body where it can freeze and split the pipe behind your wall. Once the tap runs dry, leave it in the open position through winter so any residual moisture has somewhere to expand.

Step 2: Fully Drain and Dry Your Hose

Disconnect the hose from the tap and from any nozzle, sprinkler, or connector. Walk the hose out flat across a driveway or sloped lawn and lift one end, working the water toward the low point — a 50-foot hose can hold well over a gallon. For retractable models, extend the hose to its full length first, then drain before allowing it to retract. Hoselink's no-burst, three-layer hose is built to resist kinking and cracking, but even the toughest hose benefits from being stored empty. Wipe the brass fittings dry, check the rubber washers for wear, and replace any that look flattened or brittle — this is the cheapest insurance in the garden.

Step 3: Care for the Reel Itself

A retractable reel houses a coiled spring, a locking mechanism, and internal seals — all of which dislike ice. Once your hose is drained, let it retract slowly and evenly rather than snapping back. Wipe down the housing, clear leaves and grit from the guide, and inspect the wall bracket for rust or loose screws. One of the biggest advantages of a Hoselink retractable hose reel is the quick-release wall mount: the entire unit lifts off its bracket in seconds, so you can carry it into a garage, shed, or basement for winter without unscrewing a thing. In genuinely mild climates you can leave the reel mounted, but a simple cover and a dry, drained hose go a long way.

Step 4: Winterize Sprinklers, Nozzles and Accessories

Don't stop at the hose. Spray nozzles, oscillating and tripod sprinklers, wands, and connectors all hold water in their internal chambers. Shake them out, work the trigger or adjustment ring a few times to release trapped moisture, and store them indoors in a labeled bin so spring setup takes minutes instead of an afternoon. Hoselink's watering accessories, garden tools, and even their solar outdoor lights all benefit from the same treatment — bring solar lights indoors (or at least clean the panels and switch them off if batteries need preserving), sharpen and oil your pruners and shears, and give hose connectors a quick rinse to clear mineral buildup. Doing this now means next season you simply clip everything back on and turn the tap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need to bring my hose reel indoors for winter?

If temperatures in your area drop below freezing, yes — or at minimum fully drain it. Trapped water expands as it freezes and can crack hose walls, split fittings, and damage the retraction spring. Hoselink reels make this easy because the unit lifts straight off its wall bracket for indoor storage.

Can I leave my garden hose connected to the outdoor tap over winter?

No. Leaving a hose attached traps water inside the spigot and the pipe behind your wall, even on frost-free taps. This is one of the most common causes of burst pipes. Always disconnect, drain the hose, and leave the outdoor tap in the open position.

How should I store sprinklers and watering accessories over winter?

Shake out all trapped water, work any triggers or adjustment rings to clear internal chambers, dry the fittings, and store them in a labeled bin indoors or in a dry shed. Rinse connectors to remove mineral deposits and replace worn rubber washers before spring.
