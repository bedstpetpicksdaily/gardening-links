First, the physics: why "CFM" alone lies to you

Marketing pages love to shout a single number. But moving heavy, wet leaves is a function of both volume (CFM, cubic feet per minute) and velocity (MPH), which is why professionals increasingly quote newtons of blowing force instead. A blower rated at 700 CFM / 180 MPH will roll a wet leaf mat; a 600 CFM / 200 MPH unit with a narrow nozzle is better at blasting leaves out of gravel and wet grass but pushes a smaller windrow. For heavy fall cleanup on a half-acre or more, you generally want somewhere north of 600 CFM sustained — and critically, sustained is the key word, because many cordless blowers only hit their headline number in a turbo mode that drains the pack in 6–12 minutes.

Giraffe Tools

Giraffe Tools is best known for the Grandfalls retractable pressure washer, but the brand has been steadily expanding into cordless outdoor power tools — leaf blowers, snow blowers, and cleaning equipment — sold largely direct-to-consumer. That D2C model is the whole story here: because Giraffe skips the dealer network and big-box shelf space, its cordless blowers tend to land noticeably under EGO and Stihl for comparable rated airflow, and kits typically arrive with battery and charger included rather than as a bare tool. Its handheld blowers sit in the mainstream homeowner range — roughly 500–700 CFM class with variable speed, a turbo trigger, and a brushless motor — which is genuinely enough for a typical suburban lot with mature trees. The practical advantages are convenience-driven: instant start, no fuel mixing, low vibration, and noise in the ~65–75 dB neighborhood, which matters if your municipality has gas-blower restrictions (an increasing number of cities do in 2026). The honest caveat: Giraffe's battery platform is smaller and younger than EGO's, so you're buying into a narrower tool family, and dealer service isn't as easy to walk into as with Stihl or Echo. If you already own or want the Grandfalls washer and a matching snow blower, the ecosystem logic gets much stronger.

EGO Power+ (LB6504 handheld and 765 CFM backpack)

EGO is the brand every cordless competitor is measured against, and for good reason. The 56V LB6504 handheld pushes a rated 650 CFM / 180 MPH with a turbine fan, and the EGO backpack models climb to roughly 765 CFM — numbers that genuinely rival mid-size gas. Runtime is respectable with a 5.0Ah or 7.5Ah Arc Lithium pack, and EGO's battery lineup is huge, so the same pack runs mowers, string trimmers, chainsaws and snow blowers. The trade-off is price. A kitted EGO blower with a decent battery commonly runs $250–$400, and the backpack versions with big packs push well past that. EGO is also heavier in hand than several rivals, and the biggest packs add real shoulder fatigue. If you want the deepest cordless ecosystem with the least compromise on power, EGO is the safest pick — you just pay for it.

Stihl (BR 800 C-E Magnum gas backpack and the BGA battery line)

If you have an acre or more of hardwood leaves, wet, matted, and knee-deep, Stihl's BR 800 C-E Magnum is still the tool that finishes the job fastest. It's rated in the ~912 CFM range with a side-mounted starter you can pull while wearing it, an adjustable tube length, and enough force to move soaking leaf mats that stall most cordless units. Expect roughly $600–$700 at a dealer. The downsides are exactly what you'd expect: two-stroke fuel mixing, 95+ dB of noise, meaningful vibration, and around 23 pounds strapped to your back. Stihl's battery-side BGA models are quiet and refined but generally sit below EGO and the better cordless units on raw airflow while costing more, largely because you're paying for dealer support and build quality. Buy Stihl gas for serious acreage or if you do fall cleanup professionally; buy Stihl battery if you want dealer service above all else.

Echo (PB-2620 and PB-580T gas backpacks)

Echo occupies the value-pro slot. The PB-580T delivers roughly 517 CFM / 216 MPH for around $350–$400, and the PB-2620 pushes about 456 CFM / 191 MPH at a lower price point — both cheaper than comparable Stihl gear while sharing the same commercial-oriented durability philosophy and a long consumer warranty. For homeowners with a big leafy lot who don't mind gas but flinch at Stihl money, Echo is frequently the smartest dollar-per-CFM buy in the category. What you give up is refinement: Echo units are loud, they need mixed fuel and annual carb attention, and the ergonomics on the cheaper models are less polished than Stihl's. They also can't be used at 7 a.m. without annoying every neighbor you have.

Head-to-head on the things that actually matter

Raw power on wet leaves: Stihl BR 800 > Echo PB-580T ≈ EGO backpack > Giraffe Tools handheld ≈ EGO LB6504. Price for what you get: Giraffe Tools and Echo lead, EGO is mid, Stihl is premium. Frequently Asked Questions

How many CFM do I actually need for heavy, wet fall leaves?

For a typical quarter- to half-acre suburban lot, 500–650 CFM sustained is enough if you work in strips and let the leaves dry a bit. For an acre or more of matted wet hardwood leaves, look for 700+ CFM — which in practice means a backpack unit like the Echo PB-580T, an EGO backpack, or the Stihl BR 800. Don't judge on CFM alone; a high MPH rating with a narrow nozzle helps break leaves loose from wet turf, while high CFM moves the pile once it's loose.

Will a cordless blower run long enough to finish my whole yard?

Not on one battery if you use turbo mode continuously. Most 56V–80V class blowers give roughly 12–20 minutes at full power and 45–90 minutes at moderate speed. The realistic solution is two batteries on rotation — budget for that second pack when comparing prices, because it can add $100–$250 to an EGO setup and often less on a Giraffe Tools or other D2C kit. Gas blowers still win outright on continuous runtime since refueling takes seconds.

Are gas leaf blowers getting banned?

In some places, yes. California's small off-road engine rules effectively ended new gas blower sales in the state, and a growing list of cities and HOAs restrict gas blower use by hour or ban them outright. If you live somewhere with existing restrictions or likely future ones, buying into a battery platform — Giraffe Tools, EGO, or Stihl's BGA line — is the safer long-term decision even if a gas backpack would clear your leaves faster today.
