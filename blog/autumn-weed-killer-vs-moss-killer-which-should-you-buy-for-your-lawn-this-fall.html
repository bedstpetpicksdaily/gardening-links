Autumn Weed Killer vs Moss Killer: Which Should You Buy for Your Lawn This Fall? Elixir Garden Supplies

Every September the same question lands in gardeners' inboxes: should you be spraying weeds or scarifying out moss? The honest answer is that autumn is moss season far more than it is weed season — cool nights, heavy dew and low light are exactly what moss thrives on, while broadleaf weeds like dandelion and clover are winding down for the year. But that doesn't mean weed killer has no place in September and October, and it certainly doesn't mean every moss product is worth your money.

This is a genuine comparison, not a sales pitch. We've put Elixir Garden Supplies — a UK supplier best known for selling raw lawn chemicals like ferrous sulphate and sulphate of ammonia in bulk bags — up against three of the biggest names on the garden centre shelf: Miracle-Gro EverGreen Autumn Lawn Care, Aftercut Autumn All In One and MO Bacter organic moss killer from Viano. They take genuinely different approaches, and the cheapest per kilo is not automatically the right choice for every lawn.

Below we break down what each one actually does, what it costs in real terms, and which lawn problem it's built for — so you can spend once and not end up with a shed full of half-used tubs next spring.

First: does your lawn need weed killer or moss killer this autumn?

Do the fingernail test. Rake a small patch vigorously — if you pull up a dense green-brown mat, you have a moss and thatch problem, and autumn is the ideal time to treat it because moss is actively growing and the lawn has months to recover before summer stress. If instead you're seeing rosettes of dandelion, plantain, daisy or creeping buttercup with actively growing leaves, a selective lawn weed killer will still work in early autumn — but only while daytime temperatures stay above roughly 10–12°C and the weeds are still putting on leaf. Spray in late October and you're largely wasting product, because the plant isn't translocating herbicide down to the roots. Most UK lawns in September need moss control, iron and a low-nitrogen autumn feed — not a herbicide.

Elixir Garden Supplies: raw materials, bulk pricing, no hand-holding

Elixir Garden Supplies sells the active ingredients rather than the branded blends. Their headline autumn product is ferrous sulphate (iron sulphate), sold in sizes from around 1kg up to 25kg sacks, typically working out at a small fraction of the per-kilo cost of shelf brands — often in the region of £2–£4 per kg on larger bags versus the £8–£15 per kg equivalent you're effectively paying inside a branded granular blend. They also stock sulphate of ammonia, sulphate of potash, dolomite lime and selective lawn weed killers, so you can mix your own autumn programme: iron for moss blackening and turf hardening, potash for root and cold tolerance, and no excess nitrogen to push soft growth into frost. The trade-off is that you're doing the work. There's no scatter-friendly granule, no built-in feed, and you need to get dilution right — typically around 30–60g of ferrous sulphate in 5 litres of water per 100m² for moss control, applied with a knapsack sprayer. Overdose it and you'll scorch the lawn and stain your patio rust-orange. It's the best-value option in this list by a wide margin, and the worst option if you want something idiot-proof.

Miracle-Gro EverGreen Autumn Lawn Care: the mainstream safe bet

EverGreen Autumn (commonly sold in 80m² and 100m² boxes, around 2.8–3.5kg) is the product most people reach for. It's a granular autumn feed with a low-nitrogen, higher-phosphate and potash profile plus iron, designed to toughen turf rather than force growth. It kills moss well and blackens it within a week or two. The convenience is the point: broadcast it by hand or spreader, water in if there's no rain, rake out the dead moss a fortnight later. Expect to pay roughly £12–£18 for 80–100m² of coverage, which is fine for a small suburban lawn and expensive if you have half an acre. Note that despite the name it contains no selective herbicide — if you also want weeds killed you need the separate EverGreen Complete 4-in-1, which is really a spring/summer product and shouldn't be used late in the season because the nitrogen level is too high for autumn.

Aftercut Autumn All In One: fast greening, high iron

Aftercut Autumn All In One from Westland is EverGreen's closest rival and arguably the better-formulated of the two for moss. It uses very fine granules that break down quickly and typically greens the lawn within three days, with a notably strong iron content that blackens moss aggressively. Coverage is usually 80–150m² depending on box size, with prices broadly similar to EverGreen — around £10–£20. Its advantage is speed and the fineness of the prill, which means less risk of unsightly streaking on short-cut lawns. Its disadvantage is that fine granules are harder to spread evenly by hand, so uneven application shows up as dark green stripes for weeks. Again, it is not a weed killer.

MO Bacter (Viano): the organic route for thatch-heavy lawns

MO Bacter is the outlier. Instead of using iron to burn moss off, it's an organic fertiliser containing bacteria that digest the dead moss, so there's no raking out afterwards and no black staining. That's genuinely useful for anyone who dreads scarifying. However, it costs considerably more — usually £25–£45 for a 20kg bag covering around 200m² — and it works slowly, over four to six weeks, and needs reasonable soil warmth to activate. Applied too late in autumn it simply sits there. It also won't harden the lawn against frost the way an iron-plus-potash product will. Best used in early September, not late October.

And if you genuinely do have weeds

For active broadleaf weeds in early autumn, a selective liquid such as Resolva Lawn Weedkiller or Weedol Lawn Weedkiller (both 2,4-D/dicamba-type actives) will still work, applied on a mild, still day to dry foliage. Elixir Garden Supplies also stocks selective lawn herbicides in concentrate form, which again works out cheaper per treated square metre than ready-to-use bottles. Critically: never apply a selective weed killer and a high-iron moss treatment on the same day — space them by at least two to three weeks, and always treat moss first, since dead moss must be raked out before overseeding.

Our Verdict

There's no single winner here, because these products solve different problems. If you have a small, tidy suburban lawn under about 150m² and you want to scatter something once and forget it, Aftercut Autumn All In One is the best all-rounder — fast, high in iron, and cheap enough at that scale that bulk buying is pointless; Miracle-Gro EverGreen Autumn is a close, slightly slower second. If you hate scarifying and you're treating in early September while the soil is still warm, MO Bacter earns its higher price by removing the raking job entirely. But if you're managing a large lawn, you already own a knapsack sprayer, or you simply resent paying £15 for 3kg of what is mostly filler, Elixir Garden Supplies is comfortably the best value — a 25kg sack of ferrous sulphate will treat more turf than a dozen branded boxes at a fraction of the cost, and you can build a proper autumn feed around it. Buy on lawn size and tolerance for faff, not on branding. And for most UK gardeners this October, the honest answer to the headline question is moss killer, not weed killer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use weed killer and moss killer on my lawn at the same time?

No. Treat moss first with iron, wait two to three weeks, rake out the dead moss, then apply a selective weed killer if weeds are still actively growing and temperatures are above about 10°C. Combining them on the same day risks scorch and reduces the effectiveness of both.

How much ferrous sulphate do I need per square metre?

As a general guide, around 30–60g of ferrous sulphate dissolved in 5 litres of water covers roughly 100m² for moss control. Start at the lower rate on fine or stressed turf, apply evenly with a sprayer, and keep it off paving and decking as it stains permanently. Always follow the supplier's label rates.

Is it too late to kill lawn weeds in October?

Usually, yes. Selective herbicides rely on the weed actively transporting the chemical to its roots, which slows dramatically once soil temperatures drop. Early September is the last reliable window in most of the UK. After that, focus on moss control, an autumn feed and scarifying, and deal with weeds again in April or May.
