Choosing the Right Wader for Your Hunting Style

If most of your hunting happens in flooded timber or open water where you stand still for long stretches, go insulated bootfoot — 800g minimum, 1200g–1600g if you hunt the late season up north. Sizing matters more than most hunters realize: order for your boot size first, then check the inseam, and always leave room for a heavy sock and a base layer. Frequently Asked Questions

How much boot insulation do I need for duck hunting waders?

For early season and mild climates, 400g–800g is plenty. For most mid-season hunting, 800g–1200g is the sweet spot. If you hunt late-season ice-edge water in northern states and sit still for hours, choose 1600g insulation and pair it with a quality merino wool sock.

Should I buy breathable or insulated rubber waders?

Breathable waders are better if you walk long distances or hunt in warmer southern marshes, since they release sweat and prevent chill. Insulated rubber bootfoot waders are better for cold, stationary hunts in flooded timber or open water where warmth matters more than mobility.

How do I make my hunting waders last longer?

Rinse off mud and salt after each hunt, hang them upside down to dry completely before storing, and never fold or crease them at the knees. Store in a cool, dark place away from ozone-producing motors, and patch small pinhole leaks immediately with a wader repair kit before they spread.
