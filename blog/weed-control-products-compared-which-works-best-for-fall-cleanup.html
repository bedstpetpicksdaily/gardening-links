Elixir Garden Supplies Weed Control Range

Elixir Garden Supplies offers a comprehensive portfolio of garden chemicals, including their selective and non-selective herbicides specifically formulated for UK growing conditions. Their products are widely stocked and designed for both domestic gardeners and smallholders. Elixir's weed killers typically come in concentrate form, requiring dilution, which extends coverage and reduces packaging waste. For fall cleanup, their range includes options for moss control and perennial weed removal. Prices are competitive for the concentrate format, with a standard bottle covering larger areas than ready-to-use alternatives. Customer feedback often highlights reliable performance on established weeds, though application timing matters—autumn application works best on actively growing weeds in mild conditions.

Roundup: The Industry Benchmark

Roundup remains the most recognized weed killer globally, owned by BASF. Their glyphosate-based formula is non-selective, meaning it kills most plants it contacts. Roundup offers both concentrate and ready-to-use formats, with the ready-to-use versions commanding premium prices (typically 30-40% higher than concentrate alternatives). The main advantage is decades of proven efficacy and availability in virtually every garden center. However, Roundup concentrate requires more preparation time, and the ready-to-use bottles are less economical for large autumn cleanup projects. Environmental concerns around glyphosate have also prompted some gardeners to seek alternatives, and EU regulations have periodically restricted its sale in certain regions.

Resolva: The Budget Contender

Resolva specializes in affordable weed control, offering both glyphosate-based and diquat-based herbicides at lower price points than Roundup or Elixir concentrates. Their ready-to-use bottles are particularly budget-friendly, often 15-25% cheaper than equivalent Roundup products. Resolva's diquat options work quickly (visible results in 2-3 days) and don't require soil absorption, making them suitable for damp autumn conditions. The trade-off is that diquat-based products don't translocate as effectively into root systems, meaning perennial weeds may regrow. For annual weeds and surface cleanup, Resolva delivers good value; for deep-rooted dock, dandelion, or bindweed, it's less ideal.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to apply weed killer in autumn?

Apply between September and early November when soil is moist and weeds are still actively growing. Late autumn (November onwards) is less effective because weed growth slows as temperatures drop. Morning application after dew dries but before evening damp is ideal.

Should I use concentrate or ready-to-use weed killer for fall cleanup?

Concentrate is more economical for large areas (saves 30-40% per application) but requires mixing. Ready-to-use is convenient for spot treatments but expensive for whole-garden cleanup. For fall cleanup covering multiple beds, concentrate like Elixir's range justifies the extra preparation time.

Can I apply weed killer if rain is forecast?

Most herbicides need 2-4 hours to be absorbed before rain, depending on the product. Check your specific product's label. Diquat-based products (like Resolva) can handle damp conditions better because they don't rely on root absorption. Avoid application in heavy rain; light drizzle after the dry-on period is acceptable.

Will weed killer harm my lawn during autumn treatment?

Selective herbicides target broadleaf weeds and won't harm grass. Non-selective products (like glyphosate in Roundup or Elixir concentrates) kill any plant contacted, so use a shield or spray bar to protect surrounding grass. Autumn is actually ideal for selective treatment since grass growth has slowed.

How long before I can clear dead weeds after treatment?

Wait 7-10 days after application before removing dead plant material. This allows the herbicide time to fully translocate through the root system. Earlier removal reduces effectiveness against perennial roots.
