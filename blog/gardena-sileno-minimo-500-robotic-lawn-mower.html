Frequently Asked Questions

Does this actually need WiFi and app control, or is that just marketing?

The WiFi connectivity is genuinely useful rather than gimmicky for this model. Scheduling mowing cycles remotely, monitoring battery life, and receiving alerts when something goes wrong saves real time. That said, the mower functions perfectly fine without ever opening the app. Think of it as optional convenience rather than essential infrastructure. For September owners planning fall maintenance schedules, the scheduling flexibility actually matters more than summer owners realize.

How often do I actually need to maintain this thing?

Maintenance is refreshingly straightforward compared to gas mowers. Clean the undercarriage every two weeks during active growing season—grass clippings accumulate despite mulching design. Replace blades every 3-4 months depending on lawn thickness and debris presence. The docking station rarely needs attention beyond clearing any leaves blocking the sensors. September is ideal timing to do a full inspection before fall dormancy begins, checking blade condition and sensor clarity.

What's the real battery life, and does it matter if I have charging concerns?

Runtime averages 60-90 minutes per charge on a 5,000 mAh battery. For yards under 3,500 square feet with weekly maintenance mowing, one charge per session usually suffices. For larger yards approaching 5,000 square feet, you're looking at multiple sessions weekly. The mower auto-docks and recharges independently, so battery anxiety isn't justified—it cycles back to the station when power drops to 20%. Battery degradation after 2-3 years is normal, which is why understanding your actual lawn size upfront prevents disappointment.

What happens if it rains or the grass gets too wet?

The Sileno Minimo 500 has moisture sensors that prevent operation when grass saturation exceeds safety thresholds. This is protective engineering, not a bug. Wet grass clogs blades and creates rutting; the mower won't run under those conditions even if you manually override scheduling. For September owners, this becomes relevant immediately—fall rain patterns differ from summer, and the mower will skip scheduled runs more frequently. Plan accordingly and adjust your app settings to skip rainy days.

Is this better than just mowing myself with a push mower weekly?

Yes, measurably so, but the answer depends on lawn condition priorities. This mower creates a consistently maintained appearance without physical labor—clippings remain on the lawn as natural fertilizer, which actually improves soil health over time. Push mowing once weekly works fine, but you're either removing clippings (removing nutrients) or dealing with visible clipping discharge. The robotic approach is different, not objectively superior; it's superior for convenience and long-term soil improvement, inferior if you prefer the aesthetic of mowed-line patterns on your lawn.
